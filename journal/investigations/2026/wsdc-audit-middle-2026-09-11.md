# Auditing written WSDC rules, 2018–2023

> Historical record. Dates, status claims, and instructions below describe the
> original work. See [current status](../../../docs/status.md) before acting.
> [Project journal](../../README.md)

Audited 2026-09-11. The scope is every historical assertion in the target that
describes 2018 through 2023, including the version list, era summary,
chronology, constants whose cited support falls in this period, and dataset
implications. The audit compares the cached primary rules and newsletters and
their text extractions, including 2018.1A/1B, 2019.1A, the 2020 file internally
stamped 2020.1A, the May 2021 addendum, 2023.1B/1C, and the
September-updated file internally stamped 2023.1C.

## Result

The point charts and almost all substantive rules are transcribed correctly.
The document is not yet 100% correct. Four statements need correction or
qualification, and two material transition facts should be added:

1. **2019-02-15 is a document date, not an expressly labeled effective
   date.** 2019.1A's page header says only “WSDC Points Registry Rules,
   February 15, 2019.” Its rounds rule expressly takes effect 2019-07-01, and
   the January 2019 newsletter says five-day reporting and late fees took
   effect 2019-01-01. The official-version table and chronology may call
   2019-02-15 the version/document date, but should not assert it as the
   blanket effective date without another primary source.
2. **The pandemic freeze was not “lifted” in the sense that the old windows
   resumed.** Effective 2023-07-05, 2023.1C removed the 36-month and 12-month
   windows altogether. It gave dancers qualified for All Star under the
   frozen calculation on 2023-07-04 a grace period through 2023-12-31. The
   newsletter casually says the freeze “was lifted,” but the operative rule
   is abolition plus transition. Replace “frozen through 2023” in the era
   summary with “freeze stated through 2023, superseded 2023-07-05 by removal
   of windows and an All Star grace period through 2023-12-31.”
3. **Separate primary/secondary registry presentation is documented only in
   September 2023.** The January rulebook made points per-role and formalized
   primary/secondary eligibility. The September newsletter says the database
   was “now” updated to track and display the roles separately. The dataset
   sentence “from 2023-01-01 the registry stores them under primary and
   secondary roles” overstates the evidence. Say the rules applied from
   2023-01-01 and the public database update was announced 2023-09-11; backend
   storage timing is unverified.
4. **“Results due within 3 days from May 2023” should use the exact effective
   date available.** The May 16 newsletter says “effective immediately,” so
   the supported date is 2023-05-16 (subject to the ordinary caveat that this
   is the announcement date). This affects both the chronology and dataset
   posting-delay bullet.
5. **Add the 2023 change from entrants to finalists to the dataset guidance.**
   Through 2023.1B, the five-leader/five-follower eligibility minimum applied
   to the competition. From 2023-07-05 it applied to the final. A historical
   validator cannot infer eligibility from entrant counts alone after this
   date.
6. **Add the mid-2023 Newcomer transition explicitly to the era summary or
   dataset guidance.** From 2023-01-01 through 2023-07-04, required advancement
   was 10 Newcomer points or 1 Novice point. From 2023-07-05 it became 1 point
   in either Newcomer or Novice, per role.

## Material facts absent or easy to misread

- The 2023.1B and 1C rules say all age-based WSDC contests must be open to
  every competitor meeting the age requirement. The draft singles out Masters
  because that is how the 2022 announcement was worded; the combined rulebook
  is broader (2023.1B/1C §3.1 #2b, p.7).
- The 2023 rulebook expressly permits multiple age-based WSDC J&Js per dancer
  while limiting skill-level entries to two, one per role (2023.1B/1C §3.2
  #3). This matters if dataset cardinality rules concern all J&Js rather than
  skill-level J&Js only.
- The 2023.1B rule required 60 unique event competitors for 2023 but still
  awarded points if an existing Registry Event missed that event-status
  minimum; it entered probation instead. That event-wide count must not be
  confused with the five-per-role contest minimum (2023.1B §1.2 #5a–c,
  pp.3–4).
- Starting 2023.1C, the tier remains calculated from unique competitors in
  each role, even though eligibility requires five of each role in the final.
  Final size does not determine the tier (2023.1C §3.3.1, p.10).
