# WSDC points rule history since 2010

Status: audited draft, 2026-09-11. Owner: Sandile Keswa. Companion to
[WSDC rules](wsdc-rules.md), which summarizes the values the pipeline
encodes. This document records the WSDC rule changes found since
2010-01-01 that affect how West Coast Swing dancers earn points, so that
registry rows from any year can be read against the rules that produced
them.

In scope: how many points a Jack & Jill placement earns; which contests,
roles, and placements earn points at all; how points move a dancer between
divisions. Out of scope: event structure (venues, time and distance,
competitor minimums, judging panels) except where it gates points.

The audit compared the claims with cached WSDC points documents,
rulebooks, and newsletters on 2026-09-11. Archive coverage is incomplete;
the absence of another revision is not proof that none existed. Written
rules also do not prove how every historical result was actually posted.
The [audit report](../research/wsdc-rules-audit-2026-09-11.md) records
corrections, source provenance, and unresolved questions. Each historical
claim carries a citation key in brackets, such as
`[P2018 p.3]`; the [sources](#sources) table maps every key to a URL and
capture. Page numbers are the document's own. Section and chart numbers
are the rulebook's own. Facts we could not confirm are marked
**unverified**.

## Recurring rules and their exceptions

These rules recur in the cited editions. Exceptions and the earliest
evidence used here are stated explicitly.

- Points are for eligible WSDC Jack & Jill contests, not Strictly,
  Classic, Showcase, or Pro-Am contests [P2004; P2008 p.2 FAQ 1;
  R2023a §3.2 #5b note; R2026b §3.2 #9]. Registry Events award points;
  approved Trial Events can also award them from January 2025 (see the
  date qualification below) [R2025 §1.3 #3a; NL2024-10 p.1].
- Only finalists earn points [P2008 p.2 FAQ 1; P2018 p.2 rule 4; R2026b
  §3.2 #9].
- A dancer who places twice in one division gets points for the higher
  placement only [P2004 FAQ 4; P2008 p.2 FAQ 3; R2026b §3.2 #10].
- Combined divisions award the lower division's points [P2004 FAQ 5;
  P2008 p.2 FAQ 4; R2026b §3.1 #7b]. Since 2024-07-01 a combined
  Newcomer/Novice contest awards Novice points and a combined All
  Star/Champion contest awards All Star points [R2024b §3.1 #7, §3.2
  #10b].
- Events over the New Year's holiday post to December of the outgoing
  year [P2004 FAQ 8; P2008 p.2 FAQ 7; R2026b §3.1 #10].
- Reporting results and paying the per-competitor surcharge are conditions
  of posting points in the 2012 and 2018 texts [P2011 p.1; P2018 p.2
  Event Director 5]. Those citations do not establish a pre-2010 start.
- The February 2015 text denies points for dancing more than one level
  above or below the qualified level [P2015a p.2 Petitions]. The 2018
  text expressly denies points for an unapproved dance-up petition
  [P2018 p.2 Petitions]. The 2020 text explicitly covers unapproved
  dancing up or down [P2020 p.2 rule 3]. The 2026 rules disqualify dancers in the wrong
  division without an approved petition [R2026b §3.2 #6b]. Petitions
  are limited to one level in the cited 2012 and 2026 texts [P2011 p.2
  Petitions; R2026b §3.1 #6a].
- The older documents record a Champions/Invitational/Pro category
  [P2008 p.1]. Invitational tracking ends in July 2017; Champions remains
  [NL2017-03 p.2; NL2017-12 p.2]. From 2020, 150 All Star points provide
  an entry path to Champions. Uniform allowed and required thresholds
  replace event-specific qualifications in July 2024, subject to the
  transition exception below [P2020 p.2 rule 10; R2024b §3.2 #10].

## Changes in order

### 2009-01-01 to 2010-12-31: the baseline

Points Registry Document updated 11.2008, effective 2009-01-01 [P2008
p.1]. This is the latest recovered points document effective before
2010-01-01; no intervening points revision was found.

| Tier | Couples  | 1st | 2nd | 3rd | 4th | 5th | Extra                  |
| ---- | -------- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | ---------------------- |
| 1    | 5 to 15  | 5   | 4   | 3   | 2   | 1   | none                   |
| 2    | 16 to 39 | 10  | 8   | 6   | 4   | 2   | 1 point 6th to 10th    |
| 3    | 40+      | 15  | 12  | 10  | 8   | 6   | 1 point, all finalists |

Chart: [P2008 p.1].

- The tier came from the number of couples. With uneven roles, the
  number of couples was the smaller role's count. Minimum five couples
  [P2008 p.1 "Points are recorded/tracked as follows"].
- Points were recorded for male leaders and female followers only
  [P2008 p.1 para 3, p.2 FAQ 2].
- Levels were "recommendations". Novice to Intermediate at 20 Novice
  points; Intermediate to Advanced at 25 Intermediate points; All Star
  entry recommended at 40 Advanced points but set by each event. A dancer
  "may stay" in a lower division until they had both the points and a
  first-place win. Events could and did vary [P2008 p.1 tables, p.2
  Recommendations].
- Novice, Intermediate, and Advanced points were "tracked" (used to move
  dancers). Newcomer, Junior, Masters, All Star, and
  Champions/Invitational/Pro points were "recorded" only [P2008 p.1
  tables and para 3].
- An event offering a single "Open" division had it recorded as Advanced
  [P2008 p.2 FAQ 5]. The recovered 2015 document omits that statement;
  omission alone does not establish rescission [P2015a p.2 FAQ].
- One Jack & Jill division/category per competitor per event was
  recommended [P2008 p.2 "In addition to the above recommendations"].
- Results were due within 10 days already in this version. The new
  chart applied prospectively; past points were unaffected [P2008 p.1].

### 2011-01-01: tiers by role, not by couple

Points Registry Document updated 8.2010, effective 2011-01-01 [P2010
p.1] (likely WSDC's "9.1.2010" version; **unverified**).

- The tier is now set by the number of competitors in each role. When
  numbers are uneven, leaders and followers can land in different tiers
  and are pointed separately [P2010 p.1 para 2 and "Points are
  recorded/tracked as follows"]. This is the rule still in force [R2026b
  §3.3.1].
- The chart awards and advancement thresholds match the recovered 2009
  version [P2008 p.1-2; P2010 p.1-2].

### 2012-01-01: revised skill levels and a 3-year window

Points Registry Document updated 12.2011 [P2011 p.1].

- The first recovered table labeled "Division / Skill Level Definition" says: Novice is
  "less than 15 Novice points"; Intermediate is 15 or more Novice and
  less than 30 Intermediate; Advanced is 30 or more Intermediate; All Star
  is 45 or more Advanced points "within 3 years prior to this event"
  [P2011 p.1 Division / Skill Level table]. The Novice threshold falls from 20 to 15; the
  Intermediate threshold rises from 25 to 30 [P2010 p.2 vs P2011 p.2 Recommendations].
- Junior is under 18 and open to all skill levels. Masters is over 50,
  often with additional skill-level requirements [P2011 p.1 table].
- The FAQ says each competitor may dance in only one skill-level
  division per event. Advancement is still headed "Recommendations",
  and the text acknowledges event variation. Novice and Intermediate
  dancers may stay until they have both the threshold points and a
  first-place win [P2011 p.2 FAQ 9, Recommendations].
- Petitions are one level up or down only. "Once you have
  competed/earned (current) points in a higher division, you should stay
  in that division" [P2011 p.2 Petitions].
- The 45-in-3-years All Star recommendation precedes the later 36-month
  rules [P2011 p.1; NL2017-03 p.2; P2018 p.3; R2023b
  Chart 3].

### January 2014: finalist points capped

Points Registry Document updated 1.2014 [P2014 p.1].

- Tier 2 finalist points stop at 10th place and Tier 3 at 15th. Dancers
  placing beyond those places in a large final get nothing [P2014 p.1
  "Should a Tier 2 contest have more than 10 finalists" and chart].
  Before this, Tier 3 paid every finalist [P2011 p.1 chart].
- Stated explicitly: male followers and female leaders are not eligible
  for points even if they make the final [P2014 p.2 FAQ 2].

### 2015-02-01 and 2015-07-01: either role, and rules with teeth

Points Registry Document updated 1.2015, effective 2015-02-01 [P2015a
p.1]; reissued with clarifications 2015-07-01 [P2015b p.1] together with
the Registry Event Requirements and an announcement letter [R2015].

- Points are recorded "for either Leader or Follower role". The
  traditional-role restriction ends [P2015a p.2 FAQ 2; R2015 requirements
  §5 #3]. A dancer enters one skill-level contest per event as either
  leader or follower where the event permits that choice; events may
  still require traditional roles [R2015 requirements §5 #2-3;
  R2015 announcement].
- One WSDC number per dancer, with points kept separately per role, but
  the total across both roles is what counts toward moving up, explicitly
  stated in July 2015 [P2015b p.2 rule 3]. The February text does not
  establish the combined-counting rule. This combined counting lasts until 2021 [P2020 p.2 rule 6;
  A2021 p.1].
- Only points from one skill-level competition per event are recorded
  [P2015a p.2 FAQ 2].
- "Recommendations" become "Rules" [P2015a p.2 heading "RULES"; R2015
  announcement]. The first-place-win option for remaining in Novice or
  Intermediate is omitted [P2011 p.2 vs P2015a p.2]. From July 2015,
  events must use the registry to place dancers, with fines of up to $10
  per misplaced competitor [R2015 requirements §5 #8; R2015 announcement].
  The points documents deny points for dancing more than one level above
  or below the qualified level; they do not state the later, broader
  no-approved-petition penalty in those terms [P2015a p.2 Petitions;
  P2015b p.2 Petitions].
- Newcomer is briefly labeled "WC101 / Newcomer" [P2015a p.1; P2015b
  p.1; removed per NL2017-03 p.2].
- The 10-day reporting deadline continues [P2008 p.1; P2015b p.1].
  If results and surcharge are not remitted within 120 days, the July
  requirements deny recording the event's results and remove its Registry
  status [R2015 requirements p.2 §2-3].
- The July requirements also prescribe whole-event result suppression,
  rejection of the surcharge, and loss of Registry status for violating
  the one-skill/one-age contest limit or dancing as leader in one division
  and follower in another [R2015 requirements p.4 §5 #2-3]. This is the
  stated penalty, not evidence that every violation was detected or punished.
- Missing the event-wide 60-competitor minimum does **not** remove that
  year's points. Results are still posted, including a failed probation
  year's results, despite loss or nonrenewal of Registry status [R2015
  requirements p.3 §4 #4a-c]. This event-wide minimum is separate from
  a contest's tier minimum.

### 2017-07-01: Invitational dropped, All Star window restated

March 2017 newsletter [NL2017-03].

- The WSDC stops recording Invitational Jack & Jill points, effective
  2017-07-01. Old Invitational points stay in the database [NL2017-03
  p.1-2 "Invitational Jack & Jills"]. From here Champions is the highest
  recorded division [NL2017-12 p.2 "Champions Level"].
- All Star eligibility reads "45 Advance points in the last 36 months".
  Once a dancer earns a point in All Star they may stay, as in other
  divisions [NL2017-03 p.2 "All Stars Clarification"]. (Replaced
  effective 2018-01-03 [P2018 p.3; NL2017-12 p.1].)
- The March newsletter also clarifies that no Newcomer points are needed
  to enter Novice and removes WC101 from the grid. It gives no separate
  effective date for this clarification [NL2017-03 p.2 "Newcomer Points"].
- A formal petition form is required from 2017-07-01; petitions are filed
  with the event's results [NL2017-03 p.2 "Petition Form"; NL2017-12 p.2
  "Petition Process"].

### 2018-01-03: the six-tier chart and the advancement chart

Points Registry Rules 2018.1A [P2018], announced at the 2017-11-25
membership meeting; database switched 2018-01-03 [NL2017-12 p.1 "New Six
Tier Point System"]. Built from an analysis of 2015 and 2016 results
[NL2017-12 p.1; NL2017-07 p.1 "Tier Point System"].

| Tier | Competitors per role | 1st | 2nd | 3rd | 4th | 5th | Extra                 |
| ---- | -------------------- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --------------------- |
| 1    | 5 to 10              | 3   | 2   | 1   | 0   | 0   | none                  |
| 2    | 11 to 19             | 6   | 4   | 3   | 2   | 1   | none                  |
| 3    | 20 to 39             | 10  | 8   | 6   | 4   | 2   | 1 point through 12th  |
| 4    | 40 to 79             | 15  | 12  | 10  | 8   | 6   | 1 point through 15th  |
| 5    | 80 to 129            | 20  | 16  | 14  | 12  | 10  | 2 points through 15th |
| 6    | 130+                 | 25  | 22  | 18  | 15  | 12  | 2 points through 15th |

Chart: [P2018 p.1 "Points Award per Tier Chart"].

- Minimum for points becomes five leaders and five followers in the
  division [P2018 p.1 "Tier Minimums and Guidelines"] (before: five
  couples [P2015b p.1]).
- A Jack and Jill Advancement Chart separates "allowed" from "required":
  Novice allowed at 16 Novice points, required at 30; Intermediate 30 /
  45; Advanced allowed at 45 and required at 60 Advanced points, both
  counted within 36 months [P2018 p.3 chart].
- All Star is no longer permanent. A dancer must earn 3 All Star points
  in a 36-month period to stay; otherwise they return to Advanced, and
  need 10 Advanced points within 12 months to come back [P2018 p.3 chart;
  NL2017-12 p.1]. The January 2019 newsletter clarified that the 36
  months run from the date All Star status was reached [NL2019-01 p.1
  "All Star Clarification"].
- Transition: a dancer who already had 15 Novice points by 2017-12-31
  could still dance Intermediate (with a petition showing the points).
  Everyone else needed 16 [NL2018-07 p.1 "Novice Competitor
  Clarification"].
- Champion points are tracked, but Champion is "a general category set by
  promoters" [P2018 p.3 chart note]. Age-based placements are stated as
  not recorded in this version [P2018 p.1 "Points Registry" note]
  (**unverified** whether Junior and Masters points were actually absent
  from the registry during 2018; they were "recorded" before [P2015b
  p.1] and are recorded again from 2019 [P2019 p.1, p.2 rule 9]).
- Formal petitions are limited to one level up or down and must be
  filed with the event report. A dancer entering a higher division
  without a formal petition earns no points if they reach the final
  [P2018 p.2 rule 3, Petitions 1-5].

The companion event rules retain the 120-day default penalty denying
recording of results and the preservation of points for events below the
60-competitor minimum [E2018 p.4 default note, p.5 Event Structure #4].
They no longer repeat the 2015 whole-event suppression clause for the
one-contest/both-role violations. That textual omission does not prove a
policy reversal [E2018 p.5 Competition Rules #1-3; R2015 p.4 §5 #2-3].

### 2019: Juniors and Masters, rounds, role switching

Points Registry Rules 2019.1A are dated 2019-02-15, but do not label
that date as a blanket effective date. Some items took effect 2019-07-01
[P2019 p.1 "Tier Size and Number of Competition Rounds"] or 2020-01-01
[NL2019-04 p.1 "New Rules as of 2019"].

- The registry tracks Juniors (17 or younger at event close) and Masters
  (50 or older) and records points for one Junior and one Masters contest
  per event [P2019 p.1 "Points Registry", p.2 rule 9].
- Tiers are explicitly per role, with the worked example still used
  today (18 leaders and 35 followers give Tier 2 and Tier 3) [P2019 p.1
  "Tiers and Points Awarded Per Tier"; R2026b §3.3.1].
- Advancement chart adds: a dancer with 1 or 2 All Star points may
  compete in Advanced or All Star [P2019 p.3 chart].
- Role switching within a contest is not allowed [NL2019-01 p.2 "Role
  Switching"]. A dancer may enter as either role where the event allows
  it, or traditional roles where it does not [P2019 p.2 Competitor 4].
- Number of rounds per tier becomes mandatory from 2019-07-01 [P2019 p.1;
  NL2019-04 p.1].
- Results are due within 5 days (was 10), with late fees, effective
  2019-01-01 [NL2019-01 p.1; P2019 p.2 Event Director 3].
- The registry website starts showing each dancer's computed level and
  eligibility flags [NL2019-01 p.1 "Check Your Skill Level"].
- The companion event rules prescribe loss of event status for surcharge
  delays over 120 days and result delays over 60 days. They do not repeat
  the earlier express denial of recording current results. Low-attendance
  and probation-year points remain protected [E2019 §§3-5]. Neither loss
  of status nor omission of an old clause proves which awards were posted.

### 2020-01-01: path to Champions and standardized callbacks

The cached file named 2020.1B internally identifies its points rules as
2020.1A [P2020]. No distinct substantive 1B points text was recovered.

- Events offering a WSDC Champion Jack & Jill must let All Stars with
  150 or more All Star points (no time limit) enter it. Not required to enter. Events
  may keep extra Champion qualifications of their own [P2020 p.2 rule 10,
  p.4 chart; NL2020-01 p.1-2 "New All Star Path to WSDC Champions", FAQ
  4].
- Prelim callback scoring standardized: Yes 10, Alternate 1 4.5,
  Alternate 2 4.3, Alternate 3 4.2, No 0 [NL2020-01 p.1-2 "New Callback
  System Standardization Requirement"; NL2022-09 p.2]. This decides who
  reaches the final and therefore who can earn points.
- The no-points penalty now explicitly covers dancing either up or down
  without a formal petition when the dancer reaches the final [P2020
  p.2 rule 3; P2018 p.2 Petitions for the earlier up-only wording].
- A dancer may compete in the opposite role in an age-based contest if
  the event permits [P2020 p.3 Competitor 4a].
- 2020-03: the 36-month windows for Advanced and All Star are frozen for
  the pandemic. Points keep accruing and none expire [R2023a Chart 3 note
  ‡ "effective March 2020 through December 2023"]. The freeze was
  extended through 2021 [A2021 p.2; NL2021-05 p.3], through 2023
  [NL2022-09 p.1 "WSDC extends freeze"]. On 2023-07-05 the windows
  were abolished, with a grace period for prior All Star qualification;
  the old expiry calculation did not resume [R2023b Chart 3 note ‡;
  NL2023-09 p.2].

### 2021: both roles at one event, points tracked per role

Announced February 2021; May 14, 2021 addendum to the 2020 rules [A2021
p.1; NL2021-05 p.3 "Non-dominant Role Competing and Tracking"]. The
exact effective day and the database deployment date are **unverified**;
February is the announcement month, not a proven day-level cutoff.

- A dancer may compete as both leader and follower in skill-level Jack &
  Jills at the same event. Optional for events [A2021 p.1].
- Leader and follower points are tracked separately. A dancer's level is
  the higher of the two roles [A2021 p.1 bullets 1-2]. Combined counting
  ends [A2021 p.1 example "Taylor"].
- The non-dominant role may dance one level down, but not in the same
  division as the dominant role. No petitions beyond one level down
  [A2021 p.1 bullets 3-4].
- One-time option for dancers whose combined points had already moved
  them up: stay up in the dominant role, or drop back for both roles
  [A2021 p.1 "grandfathered"].
- 2021 only: events need 60 unique competitors instead of 80 [A2021 p.2;
  NL2021-05 p.3]. The addendum also says no new Trial Events will be
  approved in 2021 [A2021 p.2].

### 2023-01-01: one rulebook, primary and secondary roles, Sophisticated

Registry Event Rules 2023.1B, dated 2022-11-02 [R2023a p.1, p.12]. First
document to combine event and points rules [NL2022-09 p.1; NL2023-01
p.1].

- Sophisticated (35 or older at event close) is tracked; one contest per
  event [NL2022-09 p.1; R2023a p.2 "Points Registry", §3.1]. The
  newsletter highlights that only a Masters contest open to all
  earns points [NL2022-09 p.1]. The rulebook applies the condition to
  all WSDC age-based contests: open to everyone meeting the age
  requirement [R2023a §3.1 #2b].
- Primary and secondary roles are formal terms. A dancer may enter at
  most two skill-level Jack & Jills, one per role, and must dance one
  level down in the secondary role. A dancer with points in both roles
  at the same level picks one role for that event. A dancer allowed to
  dance up in the primary role and choosing to do so dances one level
  below that in the secondary role [R2023a §3.2 #3, #7a-c; NL2022-09
  p.1].
- The advancement chart applies per role: "the points required for
  moving up (or down) is based on the points in either the dancer's
  primary or secondary role, not their combined points" [R2023a Chart 3
  note †].
- Newcomer gains a rule: allowed to move up at will; required to move up
  with 10 or more Newcomer points or 1 Novice point [R2023a Chart 3
  Newcomer row].
- Tier 3 finalist points stop at 10th place [R2023a Chart 4] (was 12th
  since 2018 [P2018 p.1; P2020 p.1; P2021web]).
- Competitors who fail to follow the Points Registry Rules forfeit
  placement points; unapproved dancing up or down is explicitly covered
  [R2023a §3.2 #1, #5a].
- Trial Events award no points [R2023a §1.3.1 #6; NL2022-09 p.1]; the same
  pre-2022 Trial point treatment is **unverified** in this account.
- Results due within 3 days, announced effective immediately on
  2023-05-16 [NL2023-05 p.1 "New rule change in reporting of scores"].

### 2023-07-05: windows removed, "one point moves you up"

Registry Event Rules 2023.1C, dated 2023-06-08 [R2023b p.1]. The
September-updated file named 2023.1D still prints 2023.1C internally;
its September 25 note identifies footnote-symbol changes [R2023c p.1].

- The 36-month windows are gone. Advanced: allowed to move up at 60
  Advanced points, required at 90, no time limit. All Star: allowed to
  move up at 150 All Star points, no time limit; the 3-points-to-stay and
  10-points-to-return rules are removed [R2023b Chart 3, compared with
  R2023a Chart 3].
- A single point in the next division now forces the move: Novice
  required at 30 Novice points or 1 Intermediate point; Intermediate at 45
  or 1 Advanced point; Advanced at 90 or 1 All Star point; Newcomer at 1
  Newcomer point or 1 Novice point (was 10 Newcomer points) [R2023b
  Chart 3].
- Advanced dancers who qualified for All Star under the freeze as of
  2023-07-04 could dance All Star through 2023-12-31 [R2023b Chart 3 note
  ‡; NL2023-09 p.2 "grace period"].
- The five-leaders-and-five-followers minimum now applies to the final,
  not the division [R2023b §3.1 #3, §3.2 #8a, §3.3.1; R2023a §3.2 #8a
  for the earlier wording].
- Secondary role: a dancer with little success in that role may petition
  to dance one additional level down. Event-specific; the secondary role
  is not a separate advancement track [R2023b §3.2 #7d; NL2023-09 p.1-2
  "Secondary Roles - petition process clarification"].
- The registry database shows primary and secondary role points
  separately from September 2023 [NL2023-09 p.2 "WSDC Points Registry
  database update"].

### 2024-01-01: housekeeping

Registry Event Rules 2024.1A, dated 2023-12-17 [R2024a p.1, p.14;
NL2024-01 p.2 "Rules 2024"]. Mostly event rules.

- Petitions may not be used to reach a tier's minimum count, and dancers
  admitted by petition do not count toward Tier 1's minimum [R2024a §3.1
  #6b-c; NL2024-01 p.2 "Petitions"].
- Events may restrict how many Jack & Jills a dancer enters [R2024a §3.1
  #2b; NL2024-01 p.2 "Restrictions on contest entries"].
- The open-to-all-skill-levels condition for age-based contests continues
  [R2023a §3.1 #2b; R2024a §3.1 #3a;
  NL2024-01 p.3 recap].

### 2024-03-27: raw-score wording

The 2024.1B revision is effective March 27 and specifies numerical raw
scores. This does not change registry point awards [R2024a, 1B §3.4
note 6].

### 2024-07-01: Champion defined by points, petitions up centralized

Registry Event Rules 2024.2A and 2024.2B state effective 2024-07-01
[R2024b p.1; R2024c p.1]. The later version list records revisions dated
2024-03-20, 2024-05-30, and 2024-07-11 [R2025 p.17]. The July 11 update
clarifies driving distance and requires unique numerical raw scores from
the Raw Score Judge; it is not just a footnote change [R2024c Definitions
and note 1].
Announced as an interim rule through 2025-06-30 [R2024b §3.2 note 3;
NL2024-05 p.3 "New WSDC Champions division rules"]; still in force in the
2026 rules [R2026b §3.2 #11].

- Champion: allowed with 1 Champion point or 150 All Star points;
  required with 10 Champion points or 225 All Star points. Events may no
  longer set their own qualifications for a WSDC Champion Jack & Jill
  [R2024b §3.2 #10, Chart 3; NL2024-05 p.3]. Events that had published
  their own definition before 2024-05-20 could ask to keep it through
  2024-12-31 [R2024b §3.2 note 3; NL2024-05 p.3]. A dancer required to
  dance Champion may not enter All Star without a petition [R2024b §3.2
  #10b].
- Petitions to dance up go to the WSDC Chief Judges Committee at least 15
  days before the event with two videos. Approval lasts six months at any
  event. A dancer who then earns a point in the higher division stays
  there. Petitions down stay event-specific [R2024b §3.1 #6d-e; NL2024-05
  p.2 "New Petition Process for dancing up"].
- Brief role switching (under eight counts) for creative purposes is
  allowed [R2024b §3.2 #3a].
- A combined Newcomer/Novice contest awards Novice points; a combined All
  Star/Champion contest awards All Star points [R2024b §3.1 #7, §3.2
  #10b; NL2024-05 p.3]. Not in 2024.1A [R2024a §3.1 #7].
- A dancer may not dance down when their own division is not offered
  [R2024b §3.1 #7a].

### January 2025: Tier 4 cap, secondary-role choice, Trial Event points

Registry Event Rules 2025.1A, dated 2024-12-12 [R2025 p.1, p.17;
NL2025-02 p.2 "Rules 2025"].

- Tier 4 finalist points stop at 12th place [R2025 Chart 4; NL2024-10 p.3
  "Tier 4 shall only provide finalist points through 12th place"] (was
  15th since 2018 [P2018 p.1; R2024b Chart 4]).
- Trial Events award points, provided the Chief Judge is WSDC certified.
  Points stand even if the event never becomes a Registry Event. The
  new Trial rules apply to Trial Events approved after 2024-10-16;
  reports, surcharge payment, and compliance are also required [R2025
  §1.3 #3-4, note 2; NL2024-10 p.1]. **Unresolved date discrepancy:**
  the newsletter says points begin 2025-01-02; the final rulebook is
  effective 2025-01-01. Do not turn either into a claim about the first
  actual registry row. The recovered 2023 and 2024 rules expressly
  award no Trial points [R2023a §1.3.1 #6; R2024b §1.3 #3].
- Secondary role: a dancer may dance one or two levels down from the
  level they enter in the primary role, at their own choice, unless their
  secondary-role points require them to move up. Petitions for the
  secondary role are no longer accepted [R2025 §3.1 #6a, §3.2 #7b;
  NL2024-10 p.3 "Dancing down in your secondary role"]. A dancer
  competing in Intermediate or higher in the primary role may not dance
  Newcomer in the secondary role [R2025 §3.2 #7b.i, #7d; NL2024-10 p.3].
- The rule that a dancer keeps one skill level across all contests at an
  event is dropped; it only affected non-WSDC contests [R2025 §3.2 note
  4].
- Tier 1 finals must have five in the final [R2025 Chart 5]. Events may
  skip a round when a role is at most 10 percent over the previous tier's
  maximum [R2025 Chart 5 note 9].
- Surcharge announced to rise from $1 to $2 on 2026-01-01 (no effect on
  points) [R2025 §2.2 note 3; NL2024-10 p.2].

### 2026-01-01: secondary-role chart

Registry Event Rules 2026.1A (2025-12-30) [R2026a], 2026.1B (2026-01-17)
[R2026b], 2026.1C [R2026c], 2026.1D (2026-05-22) [R2026d]. These
dates are from the filenames/publication context; the PDF headers identify
the versions. The WSDC rules index gives 2026-01-01 as the annual
effective date [W2026]. The PDFs retain a conflicting final footer saying
January 1, 2025.

No points-rule change was found across the recovered A–D versions. D
repairs the truncated Secondary Chief Judge definition and makes wording
and layout corrections [R2026a; R2026b; R2026c; R2026d]. The primary-role
advancement thresholds and tier award chart match 2025; the secondary-role
chart is new [R2026b Charts 3-5; R2025 Charts 3-4].

- A Secondary Role Advancement Chart sets the secondary level from the
  highest level the dancer is allowed in the primary role, regardless of
  which primary level they enter. Two levels down are allowed until the
  secondary role reaches its own "may move up" threshold, after which
  only one level down. Petitions down are not allowed for the secondary
  role; petitions up are [R2026b §3.2 #6a, #8a-d, Chart 4].
- With no points in either role, a dancer may enter Newcomer in either
  role. Otherwise secondary-role Newcomer requires competing in Novice
  in the primary role with fewer than 16 Novice points, and no
  secondary-role points [R2026b §3.2 #8e].
- Combined skill-level divisions may not be offered at registration. If a
  division misses Tier 1 at registration close, the event may merge it
  with the adjacent division or cancel it. The combined contest awards
  the lower division's points, except Newcomer/Novice awards Novice
  points [R2026b §3.1 #7a-b].
- Dancing in the wrong division without an approved petition is a
  disqualification [R2026b §3.2 #6b].
- Per-competitor surcharge is $2 [R2026b §2.2; NL2024-10 p.2].

## What this means for the dataset

These are project implications, not additional WSDC rules. A written rule
states what should be posted; registry observations establish what was
posted. This audit does not validate every registry row or change the
pipeline implementation.

- `points_matches_expected` in [data model](data-model.md#tables) needs an
  era-specific chart. The chart in [WSDC rules](wsdc-rules.md) matches
  the 2025 points chart [R2025 Chart 4]. The 2018–2022 rules paid Tier 3
  finalists through 12th [P2018 p.1; P2021web]; 2023–2024 paid through
  10th but still paid Tier 4 through 15th [R2023a Chart 4; R2024b
  Chart 4]. Before 2018-01-03 there were three tiers, with Tier 3's
  finalist cap appearing in the January 2014 document [P2011 p.1;
  P2014 p.1]. Before 2011-01-01 the tier used couples/the smaller role;
  afterward it used each role's competitor count [P2008 p.1; P2010 p.1].
- A historical check needs the applicable chart, actual role counts, and
  evidence of which rules governed the contest. An event end date or
  registry month alone may not settle a transition. New Year events post
  to outgoing December, but a posting-month rule does not itself state
  which rulebook governs a cross-year contest [P2008 p.2 FAQ 7; R2026b
  §3.1 #10]. Leave the flag null when required inputs or applicability
  are uncertain. There is no rule-based reason to make every pre-2018
  value null if the necessary evidence exists.
- From 2023-07-05, five unique leaders and five unique followers are
  required **in the final**, while the award tier still uses the whole
  competition's unique competitors per role [R2023b §3.2 #8a, §3.3.1].
  Entrant counts alone cannot prove that minimum was met. Petitioned
  dancers do not count toward the Tier 1 minimum under the 2024 rule
  [R2024a §3.1 #6b-c].
- Before 2015-02-01 the written rule restricted points to male leaders
  and female followers [P2014 p.2 FAQ 2]. Do not infer gender from names
  or dance roles to enforce that historical restriction. A nontraditional
  contest result could exist without being eligible for WSDC points.
- The February 2015 document records at most one skill-level result per
  dancer per event [P2015a p.2 FAQ 2]. Both-role entry was announced in
  February 2021 and documented in the May 14 addendum; the exact
  implementation date is **unverified** [A2021 p.1]. Identity linking
  must allow one dancer or bib in two skill-level contests from that
  transition onward. Age-based entries are additional: the 2023 rules
  allow multiple age-based contests per dancer [R2023a §3.2 #3].
- The 2023 rules use primary and secondary roles from January [R2023a
  §3.2]. A separate public display of their points was announced in
  September [NL2023-09 p.2]. This does not prove a January backend
  schema migration.
- Juniors and Masters were recorded before 2018 [P2015b p.1]. The 2018
  text excludes age-based placements, but actual 2018 registry behavior
  is **unverified**. The 2019 rules record them again [P2019 p.1-2].
  Sophisticated starts in 2023, when all WSDC age-based contests must be
  open to everyone meeting the age requirement [R2023a §3.1 #2].
  Invitational tracking ends 2017-07-01; old awards remain [NL2017-03 p.2].
- Under the documented 2023–2024 rules, Trial results without registry
  points are expected; those rules expressly award no Trial points [R2023a §1.3.1 #6; R2024b §1.3 #3]. For the 2025 transition,
  preserve the approval-date condition and the January 1/January 2
  discrepancy above. A rule's effective date does not prove the first
  actual Trial Event registry row [R2025 §1.3 note 2; NL2024-10 p.1].
- Missing points can reflect an explicit event-level penalty, not just
  missing source coverage. Conversely, low attendance can cost an event
  its status while preserving its points. The 2015 and 2018 requirements
  state both outcomes [R2015 §§2-5; E2018 pp.4-5]. Preserve evidence of
  the actual decision; do not infer suppression from attendance alone.
- The meaning of `adv_sliding` and `as_sliding` remains **unverified**.
  Their names suggest historical sliding windows, but the rules do not
  document the JSON fields or promise they become empty after a date.
  Do not derive API behavior from the rule change [P2018 p.3; R2023b
  Chart 3].
- Historical division inference needs more than a threshold. The
  2009–2014 documents call advancement recommendations, permit event
  variation, and allow Novice/Intermediate dancers to stay until both
  the threshold and a first-place win [P2008 p.2; P2011 p.2; P2014 p.2].
  The February 2015 text changes to rules and omits that option; July
  2015 expressly mandates registry-based placement [P2015a p.2; R2015].
  Petitions and later primary/secondary role rules also affect eligibility.
- Newcomer advancement changes within 2023: 10 Newcomer points or 1
  Novice point from January 1, then 1 Newcomer or Novice point from July
  5, per role [R2023a Chart 3; R2023b Chart 3].
- Submission deadlines were 10 days in the 2009–2018 documents [P2008
  p.1; P2018 p.2], 5 days from 2019-01-01 [NL2019-01 p.1], and 3 days
  from 2023-05-16 [NL2023-05 p.1]. These are reporting deadlines, not
  minimum delays or guarantees of registry publication. They do not
  establish polling intervals for [scheduling](scheduling.md).
