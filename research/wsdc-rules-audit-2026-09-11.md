# WSDC rules history audit

The original `design/wsdc-rules-history.md` was not fully correct. Its main
points charts were largely accurate, but several statements changed the
meaning of a rule, assigned an unsupported effective date, or treated an
inference about the registry as a documented fact. The corrected history
retains supported values and labels unresolved boundaries.

A claim of complete historical coverage is still unsupported. Available
archives do not prove that every amendment was recovered, and rulebooks
cannot establish how every historical result was actually entered in the
registry. The result is an audited account of the available evidence, not a
certification of every past registry row.

## Coverage and evidence

The review covers the history's introduction, version inventory, era table,
recurring rules, chronological claims, source descriptions, and dataset
implications. Its cutoff is September 11, 2026. The source inventory contains
54 cached PDF/HTML files, including duplicate copies, companion event rules,
and a draft; this is not a count of 54 distinct operative rulebooks.

The detailed claim ledgers record the original claims and their dispositions.
They describe defects in the pre-audit text; the corrected design document
incorporates those findings.

## Substantive corrections

### Early rules and enforcement

The 2009 recommendation concerns one Jack & Jill division **per competitor**
per event. It does not say events should offer only one contest. The 2012 and
2014 documents still frame advancement as recommendations and acknowledge
local variation. They also allow Novice and Intermediate dancers to remain
until they have both the threshold points and a first-place win. The February
2015 document changes the heading to rules and omits that retention option;
July 2015 clearly mandates registry-based placement and establishes event
penalties.[^2][^3]

The original statement that thresholds “drop” from 20 and 25 to 15 and 30 was
wrong: one falls and the other rises. The 2012 Masters definition also permits
additional skill requirements, unlike the all-skill-level Junior definition.
Those distinctions are now explicit.[^2]

The 2015 no-points penalty was materially misquoted. Its wording concerns
competing more than one level above or below the qualified division. The 2018
text expressly denies points for dancing up without a formal petition. The
2026 wrong-division rule requires disqualification absent an approved
petition. These provisions cannot be collapsed into one unchanged penalty
starting in February 2015.[^3][^4][^5]

Separate role totals combined for advancement are explicit in July 2015.
The February document establishes either-role eligibility but does not
independently prove when combined counting began. The corrected chronology
separates those facts.[^3]

The July 2015 event requirements contain additional point gates omitted
from the original history. Results and surcharge overdue by 120 days cause
non-recording of the event's results. The one-skill/one-age entry limit and
opposite-role restriction have express whole-event suppression penalties.
Conversely, falling below the event-wide 60-competitor minimum preserves that
year's points, including a failed probation year's results. The 2018 event
rules retain the default and low-attendance provisions; later omission of
particular penalty wording does not itself prove a policy reversal.[^3][^13]

### Effective dates and registry behavior

The January 2014 document supplies a month, not a day. The February 15, 2019
header supplies a document date, not a blanket effective date for all its
provisions. The January 2019 newsletter expressly puts five-day reporting and
late fees into effect on January 1. The May 16, 2023 newsletter makes three-day
reporting effective immediately.[^6][^7]

The 2021 addendum says both-role entry was announced in February; it is dated
May 14. Neither statement establishes an exact implementation day. Likewise,
the January 2023 role rules do not prove a January database migration. The
separate public primary/secondary display is documented by the September
newsletter.[^8][^9]

July 5, 2023 abolished the time windows. It did not restart the old rolling
expiry calculation after the pandemic freeze. A grace period preserved All
Star entry through December 31 for dancers qualified under the frozen
calculation on July 4.[^9]

### Recent rules and source versions

The original “Registry Events only” constant conflicts with the 2025 Trial
Event change. The 2025 rulebook allows Trial points and applies its new Trial
rules to events approved after October 16, 2024. Certification, reporting,
surcharge, and compliance conditions still apply. The newsletter announces a
January 2, 2025 start, while the final rulebook carries a January 1 effective
date. That one-day discrepancy remains unresolved.[^10]

The original claim that Champions was unreachable through points until 2024
also conflicts with its own chronology. From 2020, events offering a WSDC
Champion contest had to admit dancers with 150 All Star points. July 2024
established uniform allowed/required thresholds and replaced event-specific
qualifications, subject to the transition exception.[^11]

The 2024.1B clarification is effective March 27. The July 11 update to 2024.2B
adds driving-distance wording and a unique numerical raw-score requirement;
it is not merely a footnote-symbol change. The file named
`2025-Registry-Event-Rules_v4.pdf` explicitly labels itself a draft and is
excluded from the operative version history.[^12]

The 2020 file named 1B internally identifies its points rules as 1A. The
September 2023 file named 1D still identifies itself as 1C, with a
footnote-symbol update. These filename labels do not prove separate
substantive revisions.[^4][^9][^11]

The March 2026 filename says 1B, but the PDF identifies itself as 1C. The
recovered 2026 versions preserve the primary advancement thresholds and tier
awards from 2025 while adding the secondary-role chart. Their final footer
retains a conflicting 2025 effective date; the official index separately
states that the 2026 rules are effective January 1, 2026. These are source
metadata defects, not evidence that the 2026 secondary rules governed
2025.[^1][^5]

## Dataset consequences

A historical points check needs the applicable chart and the relevant field
counts. The corrected document explains the three-tier/six-tier transition,
2014 Tier 3 cap, 2023 Tier 3 cap reduction, and 2025 Tier 4 cap reduction. From
July 5, 2023, eligibility requires five unique competitors of each role **in
the final**, while the award tier still comes from the division's unique
competitors per role. Final size and field size serve different purposes.[^4][^9][^10]

The rule assigning New Year events to outgoing December describes registry
reporting. It does not, by itself, settle which rulebook governed every
cross-year contest. Neither the event end date nor the assigned registry
month should silently resolve an uncertain transition. A null validation
result is appropriate whenever applicability or required counts cannot be
established.[^2][^5]

The reporting deadlines are not minimum waiting periods or guarantees of
public visibility. The history now distinguishes reporting from publication.
It also removes the prediction that `adv_sliding` and `as_sliding` must become
empty after mid-2023: no reviewed rules document specifies those JSON fields.

The audit concerns documentation. It does not implement historical chart
selection, resolve all companion-document drift, or validate the entire
registry. In particular, the companion [encoded-rules document](../design/wsdc-rules.md)
still contains an older historical summary and must not override the audited
history's qualifications.

## Unresolved questions

| Question | Evidence limit | What would resolve it |
|---|---|---|
| Does the recovered August 2010 update equal the later-listed September 1 version? | Similar chronology does not establish identical revisions. | A distinct dated copy or WSDC confirmation |
| Were there additional operative revisions between recovered snapshots? | Archive silence cannot prove completeness. | Further contemporaneous documents or a complete official amendment register |
| Which exact day in January 2014 governs its change? | The document gives only `1.2014`. | A dated announcement or WSDC clarification |
| When did both-role entry and separate advancement calculation actually begin in 2021? | February announcement and May addendum do not specify an exact deployment date. | The original effective-date announcement or contemporaneous implementation evidence |
| Were Junior/Masters points actually absent in 2018? | The 2018 text excludes age-based placements, unlike earlier and later texts. | Historical registry observations or an official explanation of the discrepancy |
| Did Trial points begin January 1 or January 2, 2025, and which event was first? | Published dates conflict; eligibility does not prove actual publication. | WSDC clarification plus a dated first Trial award observation |
| Which rules governed a contest spanning a change boundary? | Posting month and end date do not fully establish applicability. | Event-specific rules/results or an official transition policy |
| What do the legacy sliding-window JSON fields mean? | The public rulebooks do not document the API. | API documentation, source code, or source-owner confirmation |
| Is every original source still available, and is the recovered 2026 sequence exhaustive? | Current direct HTTP requests failed; indexed discovery is incomplete evidence. | Successful current-source retrieval and a complete version inventory |

The incidental claim that 76 All Stars were demoted was removed. It is not a
rule, was attributed to a third-party analysis, and was not independently
reproduced. Removing it avoids presenting an unverified empirical count as
part of a primary-source rule history.
