# Audit of `design/wsdc-rules-history.md`: baseline through 2017

Status: exhaustive claim audit, 2026-09-11. Scope: the introduction,
official-version rows through 2015, the era-summary rows through 2017, the
“Constants since before 2010” claims insofar as early documents support them,
the detailed 2009–2017 chronology, and the resulting dataset claims.

## Method and evidence limits

The audit is based on the actual WSDC files rather than the existing history's
narrative. The primary evidence is the Wayback capture of the 2004 HTML, the seven archived
points PDFs (2009, 2011, 2012, 2014, February 2015, July 2015, and 2018), the
July 2015 event requirements and announcement, and the March, July, and
December 2017 newsletters. Text extraction was checked against the PDFs where
layout or a chart mattered.

## Material corrections

1. **The one-contest rule is misstated for 2009–2010 and overstated for
   2012–2014.** The 2009 and 2011 documents recommend that *a competitor* be
   allowed to dance in one Jack & Jill division/category per event. They do not
   recommend that an event offer “one skill-level Jack & Jill.” The 2012 and
   2014 FAQs say each competitor may dance in one skill-level division, but the
   documents still call the level scheme recommendations and tell readers event
   rules may vary. The July 2015 announcement and requirements provide the
   first unambiguous mandatory, enforceable version in the recovered corpus.

   Replacement: “The WSDC recommended that each competitor be allowed to enter
   only one Jack & Jill division/category per event.” For 2012–2014: “The FAQ
   stated that each competitor may dance in only one skill-level division;
   recovered documents do not establish an enforcement mechanism until July
   2015.”

2. **The ‘Constants since before 2010’ heading is false as written.** The list
   includes payment-dependent posting first evidenced in the recovered 2012
   document, the one-level petition limit first evidenced in 2012, and a
   no-points consequence introduced in February 2015. Invitational points also
   ceased in 2017, so the broad top-division claim is not constant.

   Replacement heading: “Long-running rules and later continuities.” Give each
   bullet its first verified date rather than asserting all held on 2010-01-01.

3. **The no-points petition claim changes the rule's meaning.** February 2015
   says a competitor who goes *more than one level* above or below the qualified
   level receives no points. July 2015 says the same, conditioned on making the
   final or placing. It does not say every competitor outside the qualified
   division without an approved petition receives no points. The 2018 rule
   explicitly denies points for an unapproved petition *up*; it does not state
   the same consequence for dancing down.

   Replacement: “From 2015-02-01, a competitor more than one level above or
   below their qualified level received no points; the July clarification says
   this applies if they final or place. From 2018-01-03, a competitor dancing up
   without a formal petition received no points if they reached the final.”

4. **February and July 2015 are conflated.** The February points document
   changes the heading from “Recommendations” to “RULES” and allows points in
   either role. The mandatory use of the registry, formal event compliance,
   and fine of up to $10 per improperly placed competitor are evidenced by the
   July 1 requirements and announcement. The history should not imply the fine
   or full enforcement regime began February 1.

5. **“Skill levels are defined by points for the first time” is too absolute.**
   The 2009 and 2011 documents already give division requirements (20 Novice
   points for Intermediate, 25 Intermediate points for Advanced, and a
   recommended 40 Advanced points for All Star). The 2012 document is the first
   recovered version with a table explicitly titled “Division / Skill Level
   Definition,” with the 15/30/45 thresholds and three-year All Star window.

6. **The role-transition dataset date is unresolved.** “From 2021-02” uses an
   announcement month, while the recovered addendum is dated May 14, 2021.
   Neither fact alone proves the rule's effective cutoff. Historical validation
   should leave the cutoff unverified unless an explicit effective-date source
   is recovered.

## Omissions worth adding

- The 2004 baseline used a single 10/6/4/3/2 chart, with finalist points
  dependent on rounds. This matters if the history explains why 2009 is a new
  baseline, even though dataset scope starts in 2010.
- The 2009 tier change explicitly applies prospectively and says past points
  are unaffected. The 2011 and 2012 changes say the same. This supports avoiding
  retroactive recomputation of stored points, but does not establish which date
  controls for an event spanning an effective-date boundary.
- The archive shows the ten-day reporting requirement already in the 2009
  document. It should not be presented as a 2015 innovation.
- The recovered 2012 and 2014 documents still call the advancement thresholds
  recommendations. The narrative currently implies they became binding in
  2012, then says recommendations became rules in 2015. Resolve that internal
  contradiction explicitly.
- In 2015, the source says role points are separately displayed but combined
  for advancement. A dataset should preserve the awarded role even though the
  then-current advancement calculation combined roles.

## Overall conclusion

The charts, numerical thresholds, role eligibility dates, finalist caps, and
2017 newsletter changes are mostly accurate. The document is not yet “100%
correct.” The material defects are the wrongly phrased 2009 one-contest rule,
the overstatement that 2012 rules were mandatory, the conflation of February
and July 2015, the incorrect paraphrase of the 2015 no-points rule, the false
“constants since before 2010” framing, and unsafe effective dating of the 2021
two-role dataset transition. Absolute completeness/first-occurrence claims also
need qualification because the recovered official-source sequence has gaps.

## Addendum: companion-rule point gates

The companion Registry Event rules contain several direct point gates that the
history omitted. These are within scope because they determine whether otherwise
valid placements enter the registry.

| Version and page | Exact rule and historical implication |
|---|---|
| July 2015 event requirements, p.2, §§2–3 | If results and surcharges were not remitted within 120 days, “the event results will NOT be recorded.” Results were recorded only after surcharge remittance. The 120-day suppression rule therefore predates the 2018 event book. |
| July 2015 event requirements, p.3, §4.4a/c | An event below 60 unique competitors still had its results posted and appropriate points awarded, including its probation-year results, even though it lost or failed to renew Registry Event status. Loss of status did not retroactively erase those points. |
| July 2015 event requirements, p.4, §5.2–3 | Violating the one-skill/one-age-contest limit, or competing as leader in one division and follower in another, caused that event's contest results not to be entered, its surcharges not to be accepted, and its Registry Event status to be lost. This is a whole-event result gate, not merely denial of the violating dancer's points. |
| 2018 event rules, p.4, Late Fee Charges Chart note | Results and surcharge submitted more than 120 days after event close put the member in default; “Event results will NOT be recorded in Points Registry.” |
| 2018 event rules, p.5, Registry Event Structure §4a/c | Sub-60 and probation-year results were still posted and appropriate points awarded even when the event lost status or was not renewed. |
| 2018 event rules, p.5, Contest Structure §§1–4 | The one-contest, one-role, and petition requirements remain, but the 2015 language suppressing all event results is absent. This textual omission does not prove that the sanction was rescinded. |
| 2019 event rules, p.4, §§2–4 | More than 120 days for surcharge and more than 60 days for results expressly cause loss of Registry Event status. The 2018 sentence saying current results will not be recorded is absent. This is a text change, not proof that late results began receiving points. |
| 2019 event rules, p.5, §5.4a/b | Sub-60 and probation-year results are expressly posted and points awarded despite probation or later loss of status. |
| 2020 points rules, p.2, rule 3 | A competitor dancing either **up or down** without a formal petition receives no points upon reaching the final. This expands the 2018 points text, which expressly named unapproved dancing up. |
| 2023.1B event rules, p.4, §1.3.1 Stage 2 #6 | Trial Event competitors receive no points. This explicit rule continues through 2024 and is reversed in the 2025 rules, which award Trial Event points. |
| 2023.1B event rules, p.9, §3.2 #1 and #5a | A competitor who fails to follow the Points Registry Rules receives no placement points; specifically, a primary-role dancer one level up or down must have an approved petition or receives no points. These clauses continue in the 2023.1C and 2024 rulebooks. |

No other recovered companion-rule clause directly suppresses or restores
competitor points. Other violations expose an event to general penalties or
loss of status without stating that the current event's placements are removed.
